Question 1 of 10
A project manager notices that two team members are in a heated disagreement about the technical approach for a critical deliverable. The conflict is affecting team morale and slowing progress. What should the project manager do first?
  • A. Escalate the issue to the project sponsor for a decision
  • B. Make the technical decision independently to save time
  • C. Facilitate a collaborative discussion between the team members to find a mutually agreeable solution
  • D. Remove the dissenting team member from the project
Answer: C

The project manager should first attempt to resolve the conflict collaboratively using the problem-solving (confronting) approach. This is the preferred conflict resolution technique because it addresses the root cause and leads to a win-win solution. Escalating prematurely or making unilateral decisions undermines team autonomy and morale. As a servant leader, the PM facilitates discussion, encourages open communication, and helps the team reach consensus on the best technical approach.

Question 2 of 10
An agile project team has completed three sprints. The planned velocity was 30 story points per sprint, but the actual velocities were 22, 26, and 28 story points. There are 180 story points remaining in the product backlog. Based on the team's average velocity, approximately how many more sprints are needed?
  • A. 5 sprints
  • B. 6 sprints
  • C. 7 sprints
  • D. 8 sprints
Answer: C

The average velocity over three sprints is (22 + 26 + 28) / 3 = 25.3 story points per sprint. With 180 story points remaining: 180 / 25.3 = 7.1 sprints, rounded up to approximately 7 sprints. Using actual velocity rather than planned velocity provides a more realistic forecast. The product owner should use this information to discuss scope prioritization if the timeline is constrained, or adjust release planning accordingly.

Question 3 of 10
A project has a Budget at Completion (BAC) of $500,000. After 40% of the work is complete, the project has spent $250,000. What is the Cost Performance Index (CPI) and what does it indicate?
  • A. CPI = 1.25 — the project is under budget
  • B. CPI = 0.80 — the project is over budget
  • C. CPI = 0.60 — the project is significantly over budget
  • D. CPI = 1.00 — the project is on budget
Answer: B

CPI = Earned Value (EV) / Actual Cost (AC). EV = BAC x % complete = $500,000 x 0.40 = $200,000. AC = $250,000. CPI = $200,000 / $250,000 = 0.80. A CPI less than 1.0 means the project is over budget — for every dollar spent, only $0.80 worth of work is being accomplished. The Estimate at Completion (EAC) = BAC / CPI = $500,000 / 0.80 = $625,000, meaning the project will likely cost $125,000 more than planned if current trends continue.

Question 4 of 10
A key stakeholder requests a significant change to the project scope during the execution phase. The change would add value but requires additional time and budget. What should the project manager do?
  • A. Reject the change because the project is already in execution
  • B. Implement the change immediately since it adds value
  • C. Document the change request, perform an impact analysis, and submit it to the change control board for review
  • D. Add the work to the project without updating the baseline to avoid showing schedule delays
Answer: C

All scope changes, regardless of perceived value, must follow the integrated change control process. The project manager should document the change request, assess its impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, and resources, and present the analysis to the Change Control Board (CCB) for an informed decision. Implementing changes without formal approval leads to scope creep. Rejecting changes without analysis may miss valuable opportunities. The PM should never hide scope additions from baselines.

Question 5 of 10
During a sprint retrospective, the team identifies that their daily standups are running too long and not providing value. As the Scrum Master, what is the best approach?
  • A. Cancel daily standups since the team finds them unproductive
  • B. Limit each person's speaking time to exactly 60 seconds
  • C. Coach the team on refocusing standups to the three key questions and taking detailed discussions offline
  • D. Switch to weekly status meetings instead of daily standups
Answer: C

The Scrum Master should coach the team on the purpose and structure of the daily standup. Each team member should address: what they completed since the last standup, what they plan to work on next, and any impediments. Detailed technical discussions, problem-solving, and status updates to management should be taken offline. The standup should be timeboxed to 15 minutes. Eliminating or reducing standup frequency undermines agile principles of transparency, inspection, and adaptation.

Question 6 of 10
A project manager identifies a risk that a key vendor may not deliver critical components on time. The probability is assessed as high (70%) and the impact would cause a 4-week schedule delay. What is the most appropriate risk response strategy?
  • A. Accept the risk and add 4 weeks to the schedule as contingency
  • B. Mitigate the risk by identifying an alternative vendor and placing a partial order as a backup
  • C. Transfer the risk by purchasing insurance against late delivery
  • D. Avoid the risk by removing the requirement for the vendor's components
Answer: B

Mitigation is the most appropriate response for a high-probability, high-impact negative risk. Identifying an alternative vendor and placing a partial backup order reduces both the probability of the risk occurring and its impact if it does. Acceptance is passive and leaves the project vulnerable. Transfer (insurance) does not prevent the schedule delay. Avoidance by removing the requirement may not be feasible if the components are critical. Risk mitigation proactively reduces the threat while maintaining project objectives.

Question 7 of 10
A project manager is working on a project where requirements are uncertain and likely to evolve. The customer wants to see working deliverables frequently. Which project life cycle approach is most appropriate?
  • A. Predictive (waterfall) with detailed upfront planning
  • B. Predictive with a long requirements gathering phase
  • C. Adaptive (agile) with iterative delivery and continuous feedback
  • D. Hybrid with a predictive planning phase followed by agile execution
Answer: C

When requirements are uncertain, evolving, and the customer desires frequent delivery of working products, an adaptive (agile) life cycle is most appropriate. Agile delivers working increments in short iterations (sprints), allowing the customer to provide feedback and refine requirements continuously. This approach embraces change rather than resisting it. A predictive approach assumes stable requirements; a hybrid approach may work but is less optimal when requirements are highly uncertain from the start.

Question 8 of 10
A project has four activities on the critical path: A (5 days), B (8 days), C (6 days), and D (4 days). The project sponsor requests the project be completed 5 days earlier. The PM can add resources to Activity B to reduce it by 3 days at a cost of $15,000, and can fast-track Activities C and D to overlap by 2 days. What should the PM recommend?
  • A. Crash Activity B only — it saves 3 days
  • B. Fast-track C and D only — it saves 2 days at no cost
  • C. Both crash Activity B and fast-track C and D to achieve the required 5-day reduction
  • D. Tell the sponsor that a 5-day reduction is impossible
Answer: C

The critical path is 5+8+6+4 = 23 days. The sponsor wants it in 18 days. Crashing Activity B saves 3 days (at $15,000 cost), and fast-tracking C and D saves 2 days (by overlapping them). Combined, this achieves the full 5-day reduction needed. The PM should present both options with their trade-offs: crashing costs money, and fast-tracking increases risk since overlapping activities may cause rework. The sponsor can then make an informed decision about accepting the cost and risk.

Question 9 of 10
A project team is distributed across three countries and four time zones. Team members report feeling disconnected and communication is suffering. What should the project manager prioritize?
  • A. Require all team members to work the same hours regardless of time zone
  • B. Establish overlapping core hours for synchronous communication and use collaboration tools for asynchronous work
  • C. Communicate only through email to create a written record
  • D. Assign a liaison in each location to relay messages
Answer: B

For distributed teams, the PM should establish overlapping core hours (a window where all time zones can reasonably participate) for essential synchronous meetings like standups and retrospectives. Asynchronous collaboration tools (shared documents, project boards, recorded video updates) enable ongoing communication outside those hours. Building team cohesion requires intentional effort: virtual team-building activities, clear communication protocols, and a shared team agreement. Forcing identical hours is unreasonable and unsustainable across distant time zones.

Question 10 of 10
A project delivers all planned scope on time and within budget. However, three months after deployment, the business unit reports that the delivered solution does not achieve the expected revenue increase outlined in the business case. What does this indicate?
  • A. The project was a failure because it did not achieve scope objectives
  • B. The project team should be held accountable for the revenue shortfall
  • C. The project succeeded in delivering outputs, but the expected business outcomes and benefits were not realized, indicating a gap in benefits realization management
  • D. The business case was irrelevant since the project delivered on time and budget
Answer: C

This question highlights the difference between project outputs (deliverables) and business outcomes (benefits). A project can be delivered successfully in terms of scope, schedule, and cost, yet fail to deliver the intended business value. Benefits realization management — ensuring the project's deliverables actually produce the expected business outcomes — is a key responsibility that extends beyond project completion. The PMI emphasizes that project success is ultimately measured by value delivery, not just deliverable completion.
