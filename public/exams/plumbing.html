Question 1 of 10
What is the minimum slope required for a 4-inch horizontal drain pipe per the IPC?
  • A. 1/16 inch per foot
  • B. 1/8 inch per foot
  • C. 1/4 inch per foot
  • D. 1/2 inch per foot
Answer: B

Per the IPC, horizontal drain pipes 3 inches in diameter and larger require a minimum slope of 1/8 inch per foot. Pipes smaller than 3 inches require 1/4 inch per foot. This slope ensures adequate flow velocity (minimum 2 feet per second) to carry solids through the pipe and prevent buildup and blockages.

Question 2 of 10
A water closet (toilet) has a drainage fixture unit (DFU) value of how many units per the IPC?
  • A. 2 DFU
  • B. 3 DFU
  • C. 4 DFU
  • D. 6 DFU
Answer: C

Per the IPC fixture unit table, a water closet (with a flush valve or tank) is assigned 4 drainage fixture units (DFU). This value is used to determine the required size of drain pipes, vent pipes, and building sewers. Understanding DFU values is fundamental to proper plumbing system design and sizing.

Question 3 of 10
What is the minimum size vent pipe that can be installed for a single water closet?
  • A. 1-1/4 inches
  • B. 1-1/2 inches
  • C. 2 inches
  • D. 3 inches
Answer: B

The minimum individual vent size for a water closet is 1-1/2 inches per code. The vent pipe must be at least one-half the diameter of the drain it serves (water closet drain is 3 inches minimum, half of which is 1-1/2 inches). Proper vent sizing ensures adequate air supply to maintain trap seals and prevent siphonage.

Question 4 of 10
At what temperature must the relief valve on a residential water heater be set to discharge?
  • A. 180°F
  • B. 190°F
  • C. 210°F
  • D. 250°F
Answer: C

The temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve on a water heater must be rated to discharge at or below 210°F and/or 150 psi. These settings prevent the water heater from reaching conditions that could cause a catastrophic failure. The discharge pipe must terminate at a visible location within 6 inches of the floor or to an approved indirect waste receptor.

Question 5 of 10
What is the minimum air gap required between a faucet spout and the flood level rim of a lavatory?
  • A. 1 inch or twice the diameter of the supply pipe, whichever is greater
  • B. 1/2 inch above the flood level rim
  • C. 2 inches regardless of supply pipe size
  • D. Equal to the diameter of the supply pipe
Answer: A

The minimum air gap for a fixture is defined as 1 inch or twice the effective opening (diameter) of the supply pipe, whichever is greater. An air gap is the most reliable form of backflow prevention because it provides a complete physical separation between the potable water supply and a potential source of contamination.

Question 6 of 10
When installing a gas pipe run supplying 200,000 BTU/hr at a maximum of 100 feet equivalent length, what is the minimum pipe size for natural gas at 0.5 inches w.c. pressure drop?
  • A. 3/4 inch
  • B. 1 inch
  • C. 1-1/4 inch
  • D. 1-1/2 inch
Answer: C

Per the gas pipe sizing table for natural gas at 0.5 inches w.c. pressure drop with a specific gravity of 0.60, a 100-foot run at 200,000 BTU/hr requires a minimum 1-1/4 inch pipe. Gas pipe sizing depends on the total BTU demand, pipe length, allowable pressure drop, and the specific gravity of the gas. Undersizing results in insufficient gas pressure at the appliance.

Question 7 of 10
What fitting is prohibited for use in a drainage system to make a change of direction from horizontal to vertical?
  • A. Sanitary tee
  • B. Long-sweep 90-degree elbow
  • C. Combination wye and 1/8 bend
  • D. Short-pattern 90-degree elbow (quarter bend)
Answer: D

Short-pattern 90-degree elbows (quarter bends) are prohibited in drainage systems for horizontal-to-vertical direction changes because they create an abrupt turn that can obstruct flow and cause blockages. Long-sweep elbows, combination wye and 1/8 bends, and sanitary tees are approved fittings that provide a gradual transition, maintaining proper flow characteristics in the drainage system.

Question 8 of 10
What is the maximum distance a P-trap can be located from the fixture it serves, measured from the trap weir to the vent opening?
  • A. 24 inches for a 1-1/2 inch trap
  • B. 36 inches for any trap size
  • C. It varies by trap size — 6 feet for a 1-1/2 inch trap, 8 feet for a 2-inch trap
  • D. 10 feet regardless of trap size
Answer: C

The maximum trap-to-vent distance depends on the trap arm pipe diameter. For 1-1/2 inch pipe, the maximum distance is 6 feet; for 2-inch pipe, it is 8 feet; for 3-inch pipe, it is 12 feet. Exceeding these distances can cause the trap seal to be lost through siphonage because the vent cannot adequately equalize pressure in the drain line at excessive distances.

Question 9 of 10
A wet vent serves multiple fixtures. Which fixture is typically required to be the most upstream fixture on a wet-vented section?
  • A. Bathtub
  • B. Lavatory
  • C. Water closet
  • D. Shower
Answer: C

In a wet-vented system per the IPC, the water closet is typically the most upstream (highest) fixture connection on the wet-vented section. The wet vent pipe serves as both a drain for one fixture and a vent for downstream fixtures. The wet vent must be sized to handle both the drainage and venting loads, and the water closet connection at the upper end ensures proper hydraulic function.

Question 10 of 10
When testing a new DWV system with an air test, what is the required test pressure and minimum hold time?
  • A. 3 psi for 10 minutes
  • B. 5 psi for 15 minutes
  • C. 10 psi for 30 minutes
  • D. 15 psi for 60 minutes
Answer: B

Per the IPC, when performing an air test on a DWV system, the system must be pressurized to 5 psi and must hold that pressure for a minimum of 15 minutes with no detectable pressure loss. The air test is an alternative to the water test and is commonly used when water testing is impractical. The low pressure prevents damage to trap seals and fittings.
