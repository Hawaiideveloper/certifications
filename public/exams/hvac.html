Question 1 of 10
Before opening a system for a major repair, the EPA requires technicians servicing appliances containing more than 200 pounds of R-410A to recover refrigerant to what level?
  • A. 0 psig
  • B. 0 inches Hg vacuum
  • C. 10 inches Hg vacuum
  • D. 15 inches Hg vacuum
Answer: B

Under EPA Section 608 regulations, for appliances containing more than 200 pounds of charge using an R-410A (high-pressure) refrigerant, technicians must evacuate to 0 inches Hg vacuum before making a major repair. This ensures maximum refrigerant recovery and minimizes venting to the atmosphere.

Question 2 of 10
A technician measures 125 psig suction pressure and a 48°F suction line temperature on an R-410A system. What is the suction superheat?
  • A. 5°F
  • B. 8°F
  • C. 12°F
  • D. 18°F
Answer: C

At 125 psig, R-410A has a saturation temperature of approximately 36°F. Superheat equals the actual suction line temperature minus the saturation temperature: 48°F - 36°F = 12°F. Proper superheat measurement is critical for diagnosing refrigerant charge and metering device operation.

Question 3 of 10
What is the primary purpose of the reversing valve in a heat pump system?
  • A. To regulate refrigerant flow rate to the indoor coil
  • B. To prevent liquid refrigerant from entering the compressor
  • C. To change the direction of refrigerant flow between heating and cooling modes
  • D. To control defrost cycle timing
Answer: C

The reversing valve (also called a four-way valve) redirects refrigerant flow so the indoor and outdoor coils swap roles between heating and cooling modes. In cooling mode, the outdoor coil is the condenser; in heating mode, it becomes the evaporator. The solenoid on the valve is energized or de-energized to shift a slide mechanism inside the valve body.

Question 4 of 10
A run capacitor on a single-phase compressor motor is rated at 45 microfarads. Using a capacitance meter, you read 38 microfarads. What should you do?
  • A. The capacitor is within acceptable tolerance; leave it in service
  • B. Replace the capacitor because it has lost more than 10% of its rated capacitance
  • C. Add a second capacitor in parallel to make up the difference
  • D. Increase the supply voltage to compensate for the lower capacitance
Answer: B

A run capacitor should be replaced when it measures more than 10% below its rated value. In this case, 10% of 45 MFD is 4.5, making the minimum acceptable value 40.5 MFD. At 38 MFD, the capacitor has degraded beyond acceptable limits. A weak capacitor causes higher motor amperage, overheating, and premature motor failure.

Question 5 of 10
According to Manual J, which factor has the greatest impact on the heating load calculation for a residential building?
  • A. Internal heat gains from appliances
  • B. Solar heat gain through windows
  • C. Building envelope insulation values and air infiltration rate
  • D. Number of occupants
Answer: C

For heating load calculations, the building envelope (walls, ceiling, floor insulation R-values) and air infiltration rate are the dominant factors. Unlike cooling loads, heating loads do not credit solar gains or internal heat gains because the calculation uses worst-case nighttime winter conditions. Proper insulation and air sealing dramatically reduce the design heating load.

Question 6 of 10
What type of refrigerant leak detection method is required by the EPA for verifying the repair of a leak on a system containing CFC or HCFC refrigerants?
  • A. Soap bubble test only
  • B. Ultrasonic detector
  • C. A method that can detect a leak rate of 1/4 oz per year or less
  • D. Visual inspection of oil stains
Answer: C

EPA regulations require that leak repair verification use a method capable of detecting a leak rate of 1/4 ounce per year or less. Electronic leak detectors that meet this sensitivity threshold are commonly used. Soap bubbles alone may not detect very small leaks, and visual inspection is not a reliable quantitative method.

Question 7 of 10
A packaged rooftop unit's economizer damper is stuck in the fully open position during winter. What is the most likely consequence?
  • A. The unit will short cycle on high pressure
  • B. The condenser fan motor will overheat
  • C. The unit will bring in excessive cold outdoor air, causing the heating system to run continuously and occupant comfort complaints
  • D. The evaporator will frost over due to low airflow
Answer: C

When an economizer damper is stuck fully open in winter, an unrestricted volume of cold outdoor air enters the building. This overwhelms the heating capacity, causing the furnace or heat section to run continuously while the space remains cold. The economizer should modulate between minimum ventilation position and fully open based on outdoor air enthalpy or temperature setpoints.

Question 8 of 10
What is the maximum allowable standing pressure decay rate that indicates a system is leak-free after an evacuation and nitrogen pressure test on a new R-410A installation?
  • A. No pressure change over a 24-hour period
  • B. No more than 2 psi drop in 4 hours
  • C. No more than 5 psi drop in 8 hours
  • D. No more than 10 psi drop in 12 hours
Answer: A

A properly assembled and leak-free system should hold pressure with zero change over a 24-hour standing pressure test using dry nitrogen. Any detectable pressure drop during the test period indicates a leak that must be located and repaired before evacuation and charging. Temperature corrections should be applied since pressure varies with ambient temperature changes.

Question 9 of 10
A split system air conditioner has a rated airflow requirement of 400 CFM per ton. The unit is a 3-ton system. What total external static pressure (ESP) should the blower deliver if the ductwork is properly designed?
  • A. 0.20 inches w.c.
  • B. 0.50 inches w.c.
  • C. 0.80 inches w.c.
  • D. 1.00 inches w.c.
Answer: B

A properly designed residential duct system should have a total external static pressure of approximately 0.50 inches of water column (w.c.). This accounts for supply and return ductwork, registers, grilles, and filters. Pressures above 0.50 to 0.70 inches w.c. indicate ductwork restrictions and can reduce airflow below the required 1,200 CFM (400 CFM x 3 tons), leading to poor performance and potential coil icing.

Question 10 of 10
Which safety control will shut down a gas furnace if the heat exchanger develops a crack?
  • A. High-limit switch
  • B. Pressure switch
  • C. Flame rollout switch
  • D. Flame rollout switch or high-limit switch, depending on crack location and severity
Answer: D

A cracked heat exchanger can trigger different safety controls depending on the crack location. If the crack allows combustion gases to enter the airstream near the burners, the flame rollout switch will trip because flames are pushed out of the combustion chamber. If the crack causes overheating in the heat exchanger plenum area, the high-limit switch will open. Both are manual-reset safety devices that require technician evaluation before resetting.
