Question 1 of 10
What is the minimum size copper equipment grounding conductor required for a 60-ampere branch circuit, per NEC Table 250.122?
  • A. 14 AWG
  • B. 12 AWG
  • C. 10 AWG
  • D. 8 AWG
Answer: C

Per NEC Table 250.122, a 60-ampere overcurrent protective device requires a minimum 10 AWG copper equipment grounding conductor. This table is based on the rating of the overcurrent device, not the conductor ampacity, ensuring the grounding path can safely carry fault current.

Question 2 of 10
A 3-phase, 208V motor has a nameplate full-load current of 28 amps. Per NEC 430.52, what is the maximum size standard inverse-time circuit breaker for this motor's branch circuit?
  • A. 30 amps
  • B. 50 amps
  • C. 70 amps
  • D. 90 amps
Answer: C

Per NEC 430.52 and Table 430.52, the maximum rating for an inverse-time circuit breaker protecting a squirrel cage motor is 250% of the motor FLC. Using FLC from NEC Table 430.250 (not nameplate): 28A x 2.50 = 70A. Since 70A is a standard breaker size, you would use a 70-amp breaker. Always use the NEC table values for FLC, not the motor nameplate.

Question 3 of 10
What is the maximum number of 12 AWG THHN conductors permitted in a 3/4-inch EMT conduit?
  • A. 9
  • B. 12
  • C. 16
  • D. 22
Answer: C

Per NEC Chapter 9 Table C.1, a 3/4-inch EMT can accommodate up to 16 conductors of 12 AWG THHN. This is based on the 40% fill rule for three or more conductors. The total area of 16 THHN 12 AWG conductors (each 0.0133 sq in) equals 0.2128 sq in, which is within the 0.213 sq in allowed at 40% fill of the 3/4-inch EMT internal area.

Question 4 of 10
Per NEC 210.52, what is the maximum distance allowed between receptacle outlets along the wall in a dwelling unit habitable room?
  • A. 4 feet
  • B. 6 feet
  • C. 8 feet
  • D. 12 feet
Answer: B

NEC 210.52(A) requires that no point along the floor line of any wall space be more than 6 feet from a receptacle outlet. This means receptacles must be spaced no more than 12 feet apart (6 feet on either side), ensuring that a standard 6-foot appliance cord can reach an outlet from any point along the wall.

Question 5 of 10
A dwelling unit has 2,400 sq ft of living space. What is the general lighting load in volt-amperes per NEC 220.12?
  • A. 4,800 VA
  • B. 7,200 VA
  • C. 9,600 VA
  • D. 12,000 VA
Answer: B

Per NEC Table 220.12, dwelling units require a general lighting load of 3 volt-amperes per square foot. For 2,400 sq ft: 2,400 x 3 VA = 7,200 VA. This value is used in service and feeder load calculations and includes all general-use receptacles and lighting outlets. It does not include small appliance or laundry circuits, which are calculated separately.

Question 6 of 10
What is the minimum working clearance required in front of a 480/277V panelboard per NEC 110.26(A)?
  • A. 30 inches
  • B. 36 inches
  • C. 42 inches
  • D. 48 inches
Answer: B

Per NEC 110.26(A)(1) and Table 110.26(A)(1), the minimum clear working space for Condition 1 (exposed live parts on one side with no live or grounded parts on the other) at 301 to 600 volts is 3 feet (36 inches). The working space must also be at least 30 inches wide or the width of the equipment, whichever is greater, and extend from the floor to a height of 6.5 feet or the height of the equipment.

Question 7 of 10
When using the standard calculation method for a single-family dwelling, how many small-appliance branch circuits are required per NEC 210.11(C)(1)?
  • A. 1
  • B. 2
  • C. 3
  • D. 4
Answer: B

NEC 210.11(C)(1) requires a minimum of two 20-ampere small-appliance branch circuits to serve all receptacle outlets in the kitchen, pantry, breakfast room, dining room, and similar areas. Each circuit is calculated at 1,500 VA for load calculation purposes, adding 3,000 VA to the service calculation.

Question 8 of 10
What type of GFCI protection is required for 125-volt, 15- and 20-ampere receptacles in a dwelling unit garage per NEC 210.8(A)?
  • A. GFCI protection is only required for receptacles within 6 feet of a sink
  • B. GFCI protection is only required for the first receptacle
  • C. GFCI protection is required for all receptacles in the garage
  • D. GFCI protection is not required in attached garages
Answer: C

Per NEC 210.8(A)(2), all 125-volt, 15- and 20-ampere receptacles installed in garages and accessory buildings with a floor at or below grade require GFCI protection. This applies to all receptacles in the space, not just those near water sources. The only exception is for receptacles that are not readily accessible, such as a ceiling-mounted receptacle for a garage door opener.

Question 9 of 10
What is the ampacity of a 6 AWG THHN copper conductor installed in a dry location at 30°C ambient temperature per NEC Table 310.16?
  • A. 55 amps
  • B. 65 amps
  • C. 75 amps
  • D. 85 amps
Answer: C

Per NEC Table 310.16, a 6 AWG THHN (rated 90°C) copper conductor has an ampacity of 75 amps at 30°C ambient temperature. THHN is rated in the 90°C column of Table 310.16. However, when used with 60°C-rated equipment terminations (common for most equipment under 100A), the conductor must be used at the 60°C column ampacity of 55A per NEC 110.14(C).

Question 10 of 10
A single-phase transformer is rated 10 kVA with a 480V primary and a 120/240V secondary. What is the maximum overcurrent protection required on the primary side per NEC 450.3(B)?
  • A. 15 amps
  • B. 20 amps
  • C. 25 amps
  • D. 30 amps
Answer: D

The primary full-load current is 10,000 VA / 480V = 20.83A. Per NEC 450.3(B), transformers 600 volts and below with primary overcurrent protection only (no secondary protection) can be protected at a maximum of 125% of primary FLC: 20.83A x 1.25 = 26.04A. Since this does not correspond to a standard fuse or breaker size, NEC 240.6(A) permits rounding up to the next standard size, which is 30 amps.
