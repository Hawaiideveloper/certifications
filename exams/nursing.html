Question 1 of 10
A nurse receives change-of-shift report on four patients. Which patient should the nurse assess first?
  • A. A patient 1 day post-cholecystectomy with a temperature of 99.2°F (37.3°C)
  • B. A patient with COPD and oxygen saturation of 90% on 2L nasal cannula
  • C. A patient with diabetes who is diaphoretic and reports feeling shaky
  • D. A patient with a hip replacement requesting pain medication
Answer: C

The diaphoretic, shaky diabetic patient is showing signs of hypoglycemia, which is a potentially life-threatening emergency requiring immediate intervention. Using the ABCs and Maslow's hierarchy, this patient's physiological stability is most acutely threatened. The post-surgical low-grade fever is expected, the COPD patient's O2 sat is within acceptable range for COPD, and pain medication is important but not emergent.

Question 2 of 10
A patient is receiving IV heparin therapy. The aPTT result comes back at 120 seconds (therapeutic range: 60-80 seconds). What is the nurse's priority action?
  • A. Document the finding and continue monitoring
  • B. Stop the heparin infusion and notify the healthcare provider
  • C. Administer protamine sulfate immediately
  • D. Decrease the heparin infusion rate by half
Answer: B

An aPTT of 120 seconds is significantly above the therapeutic range (60-80 seconds), indicating the patient is over-anticoagulated and at high risk for bleeding. The priority action is to stop the heparin infusion and notify the healthcare provider for further orders. Protamine sulfate (the heparin antidote) should only be administered with a provider's order. The nurse should not independently adjust medication dosages.

Question 3 of 10
A nurse is caring for a patient with a potassium level of 6.2 mEq/L (normal: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L). Which ECG finding should the nurse expect?
  • A. Flattened T waves and prominent U waves
  • B. Prolonged QT interval
  • C. Tall, peaked T waves and widened QRS complex
  • D. ST segment depression
Answer: C

Hyperkalemia (K+ above 5.0 mEq/L) causes characteristic ECG changes including tall, peaked (tented) T waves, widened QRS complexes, and flattened P waves. At levels above 6.0, the patient is at risk for fatal cardiac dysrhythmias. Flattened T waves and U waves are seen in hypokalemia, not hyperkalemia. Treatment includes IV calcium gluconate (to stabilize the myocardium), insulin with dextrose, and sodium polystyrene sulfonate.

Question 4 of 10
An RN is delegating tasks to a licensed practical nurse (LPN) and an unlicensed assistive personnel (UAP). Which task is appropriate to delegate to the UAP?
  • A. Administering oral medications to a stable patient
  • B. Performing a focused respiratory assessment
  • C. Reinserting a Foley catheter that was accidentally dislodged
  • D. Obtaining vital signs on a stable postoperative patient
Answer: D

Obtaining vital signs on a stable patient is within the UAP's scope of practice. Medication administration (even oral) requires a licensed nurse (LPN or RN). Assessment is an RN responsibility that cannot be delegated. Catheter insertion is a sterile procedure requiring a licensed nurse. When delegating, the RN must consider the five rights of delegation: right task, right circumstances, right person, right direction/communication, and right supervision.

Question 5 of 10
A patient with heart failure is prescribed furosemide (Lasix) 40 mg IV push. Which laboratory value should the nurse monitor most closely?
  • A. Serum sodium
  • B. Serum potassium
  • C. Blood urea nitrogen (BUN)
  • D. Serum calcium
Answer: B

Furosemide is a loop diuretic that inhibits sodium and chloride reabsorption in the loop of Henle, causing significant potassium wasting. Hypokalemia is the most dangerous adverse effect because it can cause life-threatening cardiac dysrhythmias, especially in a patient already on digoxin (common in heart failure). The nurse should monitor potassium levels before and after administration and assess for signs of hypokalemia such as muscle weakness, leg cramps, and irregular pulse.

Question 6 of 10
A patient is admitted with suspected bacterial meningitis. Which isolation precautions should the nurse implement?
  • A. Contact precautions with gown and gloves
  • B. Airborne precautions with an N95 respirator
  • C. Droplet precautions with a surgical mask
  • D. Standard precautions only
Answer: C

Bacterial meningitis (particularly Neisseria meningitidis) is transmitted via respiratory droplets, requiring droplet precautions. This includes a private room, surgical mask when within 3 feet of the patient, and maintaining the door closed. Droplet precautions should remain in place until the patient has received appropriate antibiotic therapy for 24 hours. Airborne precautions (N95) are reserved for diseases like tuberculosis, measles, and varicella.

Question 7 of 10
A patient with a chest tube has continuous bubbling in the water seal chamber. What does this finding indicate?
  • A. The lung has fully re-expanded
  • B. The suction is set at the correct level
  • C. There is an air leak in the system
  • D. This is a normal expected finding
Answer: C

Continuous bubbling in the water seal chamber indicates an air leak. Intermittent bubbling with respiration is normal and expected, especially in the first 24-48 hours when the pneumothorax is resolving. Continuous bubbling suggests a leak either at a connection point in the tubing system, at the chest tube insertion site, or from a persistent bronchopleural fistula. The nurse should check all connections, assess the insertion site dressing, and notify the healthcare provider if the leak persists.

Question 8 of 10
A nurse is preparing to administer a blood transfusion. After verifying the blood product with another nurse, what is the appropriate initial infusion rate?
  • A. Slowly at 2 mL/min for the first 15 minutes, then increase if no reaction occurs
  • B. At the prescribed rate immediately since the blood was already verified
  • C. Wide open for the first 50 mL to ensure the line is patent
  • D. At 125 mL/hr throughout the entire transfusion
Answer: A

Blood transfusions should be initiated slowly (approximately 2 mL/min or 25-50 mL for the first 15 minutes) while the nurse remains with the patient to monitor for transfusion reactions. Most severe reactions (hemolytic, anaphylactic) occur within the first 15 minutes. After 15 minutes without signs of reaction, the rate can be increased to the prescribed rate. The entire unit must be infused within 4 hours to prevent bacterial growth. Vital signs should be taken before, 15 minutes into, and after the transfusion.

Question 9 of 10
A patient with type 1 diabetes is found unresponsive. The blood glucose reading is 38 mg/dL. What is the nurse's priority intervention?
  • A. Administer oral glucose gel between the cheek and gum
  • B. Give the patient orange juice with sugar packets
  • C. Administer IV dextrose 50% (D50) as prescribed
  • D. Administer the patient's scheduled insulin and recheck in 30 minutes
Answer: C

For an unresponsive patient with severe hypoglycemia (blood glucose 38 mg/dL), IV dextrose 50% is the priority intervention because the patient cannot safely swallow oral glucose. Oral glucose or juice is contraindicated in an unresponsive patient due to aspiration risk. Administering insulin would further lower blood glucose and is dangerous. If IV access is unavailable, glucagon 1 mg IM is the alternative. After administration, blood glucose should be rechecked within 15 minutes.

Question 10 of 10
A postpartum patient delivered 2 hours ago and the nurse finds the uterine fundus boggy and displaced to the right. What is the nurse's first action?
  • A. Administer oxytocin (Pitocin) as prescribed
  • B. Notify the healthcare provider immediately
  • C. Have the patient empty her bladder and then massage the fundus
  • D. Apply ice packs to the lower abdomen
Answer: C

A boggy fundus displaced to the right indicates uterine atony complicated by a distended bladder. The full bladder pushes the uterus to the side and prevents it from contracting effectively. The first intervention is to have the patient void (or catheterize if unable to void) to empty the bladder, then perform fundal massage to stimulate uterine contraction. If the fundus remains boggy after bladder emptying and massage, then oxytocin administration and provider notification are appropriate next steps.
