Question 1 of 10
During a pre-trip inspection of an air brake system, you should pump the brake pedal to reduce air pressure. At what psi should the low-pressure warning device activate?
  • A. 40 psi
  • B. 50 psi
  • C. 60 psi
  • D. 80 psi
Answer: C

The low-pressure warning device (light, buzzer, or wig-wag) must activate before the air pressure drops below 60 psi. This warning alerts the driver that air pressure is getting dangerously low and the spring brakes may apply automatically. During a pre-trip inspection, you should fan the brakes to reduce pressure and verify the warning activates at or before 60 psi. If it does not, the vehicle should not be driven.

Question 2 of 10
What is the maximum allowable air pressure loss rate for a combination vehicle with the engine off and brakes fully applied?
  • A. 2 psi per minute
  • B. 3 psi per minute
  • C. 4 psi per minute
  • D. 5 psi per minute
Answer: C

For a combination vehicle (tractor-trailer), the maximum allowable air loss rate is 4 psi per minute with the engine off, brakes fully applied, and after initial pressure drop stabilizes. For a single vehicle, the maximum is 3 psi per minute. A loss rate exceeding these limits indicates an air leak that must be repaired before operating the vehicle. This is part of the applied-pressure leakage test during pre-trip inspection.

Question 3 of 10
You are driving a tractor-trailer combination on a wet road. You apply the brakes hard and the drive wheels lock up. What is the most likely result?
  • A. The trailer will swing out to the side (trailer jackknife)
  • B. The tractor will swing out to the side (tractor jackknife)
  • C. The vehicle will stop in a straight line
  • D. The trailer brakes will automatically release
Answer: B

When the drive wheels lock up, the rear of the tractor tends to swing out because the locked tires lose traction and the trailer pushes the tractor. This is a tractor jackknife. The trailer stays in a straight line while the tractor swings sideways, forming a V-shape. To correct, release the brakes to allow the drive wheels to regain traction. Anti-lock braking systems (ABS) help prevent wheel lockup but the driver should still use controlled braking techniques.

Question 4 of 10
Under FMCSA hours of service regulations, what is the maximum number of hours a property-carrying CMV driver can drive after 10 consecutive hours off duty?
  • A. 8 hours
  • B. 10 hours
  • C. 11 hours
  • D. 14 hours
Answer: C

Under FMCSA hours of service rules for property-carrying drivers, you may drive a maximum of 11 hours after 10 consecutive hours off duty. Additionally, you cannot drive beyond the 14th consecutive hour after coming on duty (the 14-hour window), and you must take a 30-minute break after 8 cumulative hours of driving. The 60/70-hour rule further limits total on-duty time over 7 or 8 consecutive days.

Question 5 of 10
When coupling a tractor to a trailer, in what position should the fifth wheel be before backing under the trailer?
  • A. Tilted forward with the locking jaws open
  • B. Tilted down toward the rear of the tractor with the locking jaws open
  • C. Level and flat with the locking jaws closed
  • D. Tilted up toward the front of the tractor with the locking jaws open
Answer: B

Before coupling, the fifth wheel should be tilted down toward the rear of the tractor and the locking jaws should be in the open position. This angle allows the trailer kingpin to slide smoothly into the fifth wheel throat and engage the locking jaws. After coupling, the driver must verify the connection by performing a tug test (pulling gently against the trailer brakes) and visually inspecting that the locking jaws are closed around the kingpin with no gap between the upper and lower fifth wheel.

Question 6 of 10
What is the primary danger of liquid surge in a partially loaded tanker vehicle?
  • A. The liquid will overheat from friction against the tank walls
  • B. The tank will develop structural cracks from repeated impacts
  • C. The liquid surge can push the vehicle in the direction the liquid moves, making it difficult to stop or causing rollover
  • D. The liquid will separate into layers reducing overall cargo weight
Answer: C

Liquid surge occurs when the liquid inside a partially loaded tank moves forward, backward, or sideways in response to vehicle movement. When braking, the liquid surges forward and pushes the truck forward, increasing stopping distance. On curves, side-to-side surge can cause rollover. Baffled tanks reduce front-to-back surge but do not prevent side-to-side surge. Smooth bore (unbaffled) tanks used for food-grade products have the most surge. Drivers should use smooth, gradual braking and reduce speed on curves.

Question 7 of 10
A hazardous materials shipment requires placards. At what minimum aggregate weight of hazardous materials in a single class must placards be displayed on the vehicle?
  • A. 100 pounds
  • B. 500 pounds
  • C. 1,001 pounds
  • D. 5,000 pounds
Answer: C

Placards are required when hauling 1,001 pounds or more of a hazardous material in a single class (Table 2 materials). However, certain highly dangerous materials (Table 1) such as explosives, poison gas, and radioactive materials require placards at any quantity, even one pound. Placards must be placed on all four sides of the vehicle (front, back, and both sides) and must be readable from 50 feet away.

Question 8 of 10
You are driving at 55 mph on dry pavement. What is the approximate total stopping distance for a fully loaded tractor-trailer?
  • A. About 200 feet
  • B. About 300 feet
  • C. About 390 feet (the length of a football field)
  • D. About 500 feet
Answer: C

At 55 mph, the total stopping distance for a loaded tractor-trailer on dry pavement is approximately 390 feet, which is about the length of a football field plus both end zones. This includes perception distance (about 60 feet), reaction distance (about 60 feet), and braking distance (about 270 feet). On wet pavement, stopping distance can double. This is why following distance of at least 6-7 seconds is recommended for commercial vehicles at highway speeds.

Question 9 of 10
When performing a pre-trip inspection, what should you check regarding the steering system?
  • A. Only check that the steering wheel turns freely
  • B. Only check the power steering fluid level
  • C. Check for play in the steering wheel (no more than 10 degrees), power steering fluid level, hoses, and linkage connections
  • D. Steering does not need to be checked during a pre-trip inspection
Answer: C

A thorough steering system inspection includes checking the steering wheel for excessive free play (should not exceed approximately 10 degrees or about 2 inches of movement on a 20-inch wheel before the front tires begin to move). Additionally, check the power steering fluid level, inspect hoses and connections for leaks, check the steering linkage and gear box for looseness or damage, and ensure the steering column is securely mounted. Steering defects are critical safety items that can put the vehicle out of service.

Question 10 of 10
Under what circumstances should you downshift on a long downgrade?
  • A. Downshift only after the brakes start to fade
  • B. Downshift before starting down the grade to use engine braking as the primary speed control
  • C. Downshift at the bottom of the grade to accelerate away
  • D. Never downshift on a downgrade; use only the service brakes
Answer: B

You should always downshift to a gear low enough to control speed before starting down a long or steep downgrade. Engine braking (the retarding force of the engine) should be the primary method of speed control on downgrades. Use the brakes only as a supplement and apply them in a snub-braking pattern (apply firmly to reduce speed 5 mph below safe speed, then release). Riding the brakes continuously causes brake fade from overheating, which can lead to complete brake failure.
